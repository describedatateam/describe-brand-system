The stars are the evidence. The swan is the story.

Al-Sufi measured every star in Cygnus and then drew a swan through them, because a picture is easier to remember than a table. We do the same with your numbers, in that order: the measurements first, and only then the story they support.

Cygnus, from al-Sufi's Book of the Images of the Fixed Stars, Iran, late 15th century. The Met, public domain.

The same stars, two views.

Al-Sufi drew every constellation twice. Before we report a number, we check which view it was measured from.

Cancer, from the same page of al-Sufi's manuscript. The data didn't change between the two drawings. The point of view did, and so did what it looked like.
