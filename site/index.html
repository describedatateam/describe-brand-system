Who we're not for

About

The name is the method.

.describe(|

In Python, .describe() is the first thing you run on a dataset you've never seen. You don't ask it anything clever. You ask it to tell you what's there — how many rows, what the average is, where the extremes sit, what's missing.

It's the moment before any analysis. Before assumptions, before a hypothesis, before someone decides what the answer should be. You look first.

That's the whole method, so it's the name. The bracket stays open because the work hasn't started yet — what goes inside it is your business.

What we believe
A number doesn't have to be wrong to mislead you.

A different denominator, a shorter window, a slightly rephrased question — and honest data says something else. Deliberately in politics; in good faith nearly everywhere else.

In a business, the risk isn't the arithmetic. It's asking the wrong question, or reading more into an answer than it can carry. So we show our method, we name what we excluded, and we say plainly when a finding is weaker than you were hoping. You should be able to check us.

And if you've analysed something yourself and you're about to act on it — have someone verify it first.
